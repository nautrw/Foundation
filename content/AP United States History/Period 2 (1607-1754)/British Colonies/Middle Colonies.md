- Generally fertile soil and moderate climate
- Known as the **breadbasket** colonies
- Exported lots of wheat and cereals
# New York
- The English peacefully took over **New Amsterdam** in 1664 from the Dutch
- **New York** was founded in 1664 when the king (Charles II) granted James II (as of then the Duke of York but he became king James II) the land
- [[Types of Colonies|Proprietary]] colony
- ==New Jersey was split away that dame year because James II thought New York was too big==
- Dutch settlers were treated well (allowed freedom of worship and to speak Dutch)
- No representative assembly for the people
- James II taxed the people without their representation
	- Caused the settlers to oppose him
	- Allowed the governor of New York to establish a representative assembly and other things in 1683
# New Jersey
- James II thought that New York was too big so he split it in 1664
- *East* New Jersey was given to Sir George Carteret
- *West* New Jersey was given to Lord John Berkeley
![The original provinces of West and East Jersey are shown in yellow and green respectively. The Keith Line is shown in red, and the Coxe–Barclay Line is shown in orange.](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/39/Wpdms_east_west_new_jersey.png)[^1]
- **New Jersey** was established as a [[Types of Colonies|royal]] colony in 1702
# Pennsylvania
- [[Types of Colonies|Proprietary]] colony founded by **William Penn (Sr.)** in 1681
- Land was left to **William Penn (Jr.)**
- He made it a safe haven for [[Religious Groups from England#Quakers|Quaker]] and other religions
- **Frame of Government** (1682-83) was a framework for a government with a representative assembly elected by landowners
- **Charter Of Liberties** (1701) guarantees freedom of worship and unrestricted immigration
- Attracted settlers by promising religious freedom and good terms for land ownership
# Delaware
- Originally part of New York in 1664
- Owned by William Penn Jr. in 1682
- William Penn Jr. granted them their own assembly in 1703
- Became its own colony in 1776
